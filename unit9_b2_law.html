الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات أكاديمية عن القانون والعدالة
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Nominative Absolutes في IELTS Writing

  • للنتائج: "The evidence destroyed, the prosecution had no case to pursue."
  • للوصف المُكثَّف: "All appeals exhausted, the defendant awaited the final ruling."
  • للسياق: "The rule of law weakened, citizens have little recourse against state power."
  • لإضافة تفاصيل: "The verdict delivered, the court fell into a profound silence."
Should capital punishment be abolished worldwide? Discuss both views and give your opinion.
هل يجب إلغاء عقوبة الإعدام عالمياً؟ (Discuss both views)
The case for capital punishment rests on deterrence and retribution: society, having tried all other measures, may resort to the ultimate sanction for the gravest crimes. Proponents argue that certain offences forfeit the right to life. That said, the evidence for deterrence is, at best, inconclusive. All studies conducted, no reliable pattern of crime reduction attributable to capital punishment has been established. More troubling still, the possibility of error cannot be eliminated: the sentence once carried out, no correction is possible. In my view, this irreversibility alone is sufficient reason for abolition. A justice system worth the name must be able to remedy its mistakes. Capital punishment, by definition, cannot.
Prison systems should focus on rehabilitation rather than punishment. To what extent do you agree?
يجب أن تركّز السجون على إعادة التأهيل لا العقوبة. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The purpose of imprisonment has long been contested, society having never fully agreed on whether prison exists to punish, to deter, to incapacitate, or to reform. I largely agree that rehabilitation should be the primary goal. The evidence having been examined, recidivism rates are substantially lower in systems that prioritise education, mental health treatment, and vocational training over punitive isolation. That said, rehabilitation and punishment are not mutually exclusive: a sentence must be proportionate to the offence, community trust in the system having been built on that proportionality. What is indefensible is a prison system that brutalises people and returns them to society worse than it found them — a process that serves neither justice nor public safety.
Is it ever acceptable to break the law for moral reasons? Discuss.
هل من المقبول أحياناً مخالفة القانون لأسباب أخلاقية؟ ناقش.
The history of moral progress is, in significant part, a history of lawbreaking. The rule of law accepted, it becomes necessary to ask: what happens when law and justice diverge? Those who sheltered refugees in defiance of unjust edicts, those who sat in segregated seats despite explicit prohibition — history having judged these actions, we now consider them not crimes but acts of conscience. The philosophical tradition of civil disobedience holds that unjust laws carry no binding moral authority. That said, this cannot license arbitrary non-compliance. Any act of conscience must be genuinely principled, publicly acknowledged, and proportionate. The law once circumvented for convenience rather than conscience, the rule of law itself — our most important protection against power — is undermined.
Wealthy individuals have an unfair advantage in the legal system. To what extent do you agree?
الأثرياء يتمتعون بميزة غير عادلة في النظام القانوني. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
Justice, in principle, is blind; in practice, its scales are tipped by wealth. The principle of equality before the law established, its application has consistently fallen short. Legal representation purchased, the quality of a defence correlates far too strongly with the financial resources of the accused. Studies conducted across multiple jurisdictions, this correlation is well documented and deeply troubling. A wealthy defendant, access to specialist lawyers secured, faces a fundamentally different trial from an indigent one relying on an overworked public defender. I strongly agree, therefore, that wealth confers an unjust advantage. The remedy lies not in weakening the rights of the affluent but in raising the quality of representation available to those who cannot afford it.
Should there be a global system of international criminal justice? Discuss the advantages and disadvantages.
هل يجب وجود نظام عدالة جنائية دولية عالمية؟ ناقش المزايا والعيوب.
The case for international criminal justice rests on a compelling premise: certain crimes — genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes — are so grave that they exceed the jurisdiction of any single state. Nuremberg having established this principle, the International Criminal Court represents its most ambitious institutional expression. Sovereignty set aside, victims of atrocity deserve a forum that their own governments cannot close. The advantages are real: accountability, deterrence, and the consolidation of international norms. The disadvantages, however, are equally substantial. The court's jurisdiction being contested, powerful states — including the United States, Russia, and China — have refused to join, limiting its reach to precisely those actors least able to resist scrutiny. International justice, all options considered, is better than its absence — but only if applied consistently.
