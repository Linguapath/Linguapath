الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات أكاديمية عن السياسة والقوة
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Relative Clauses في IELTS Writing

  • Non-defining في وصف الحجج: "Democracy, which requires an informed electorate, is under threat."
  • Whose وwhom في السياق الرسمي: "Leaders whose mandate is questioned... / The voters whom politicians ignore..."
  • Preposition + which: "The system in which we live / The parliament through which laws pass"
  • Reduced relative: "Politicians elected by narrow margins tend to avoid bold reform."
Some argue that democracy is the best system of government. Others believe it has serious weaknesses. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
ناقش مزايا وعيوب الديمقراطية وأعطِ رأيك. (Discuss both views)
Democracy, which rests on the idea that governments derive their authority from the governed, has much to recommend it. In systems in which citizens elect their representatives, leaders remain accountable and power is peacefully transferred. The voters whom politicians serve have a mechanism, however imperfect, to remove those who fail them. Critics, whose concerns deserve serious consideration, point to the tyranny of majorities, the short-termism of electoral cycles, and the ease with which populism — a force that exploits fear rather than reason — can capture democratic institutions. In my view, democracy, for all its imperfections, remains preferable to the alternatives. What is needed is not less democracy but better-informed, more actively engaged citizens.
Corruption is a major problem in many countries. What are its causes, and how can it be reduced?
ما أسباب الفساد وكيف يمكن تقليله؟ (Problem/Solution)
Corruption, which can be defined as the abuse of entrusted power for private gain, thrives where accountability is weak and transparency absent. Politicians who face no meaningful scrutiny, officials whose decisions are unreviewable, and institutions in which secrecy is the norm all create conditions in which corrupt practices spread. The causes are structural: low public-sector wages that push officials toward bribery, weak judiciaries whose independence is compromised, and media landscapes in which investigative journalism is suppressed. Solutions require building the institutions whose strength alone can deter wrongdoing: independent courts, protected whistleblowers, and a free press — all of which require sustained political will to establish and maintain.
Some believe governments should prioritise national interests over international cooperation. Do you agree?
هل يجب أن تُقدّم الحكومات مصالحها الوطنية على التعاون الدولي؟ (Opinion)
The tension between national interest and international cooperation is one in which modern politics is constantly trapped. Leaders whose mandate comes from domestic electorates face pressure to prioritise immediate national concerns. Yet the challenges that most urgently require attention — climate change, pandemics, financial instability — are precisely those in which no single country can succeed alone. I largely disagree with the premise that national interest and international cooperation are opposed. Trade agreements, whose benefits are widely distributed, strengthen economies whose citizens they serve. The world in which we live is one of interdependence that makes pure nationalism not just morally narrow but economically self-defeating.
Is freedom of the press essential for a healthy democracy?
هل حرية الصحافة ضرورية للديمقراطية الصحية؟ (Opinion)
A free press, whose primary function is to hold power to account, is not merely a feature of democracy — it is among its essential preconditions. Citizens who cannot access reliable information about the decisions made in their name cannot meaningfully exercise political judgement. Governments whose actions go unreported operate without constraint. That said, a press in which ownership is concentrated in few hands, or one whose funding depends entirely on advertising, faces its own structural pressures that compromise independence. The solution is not censorship — which invariably serves those already in power — but rather media literacy education and regulatory frameworks in which plurality and independence are protected.
Rising political polarisation is a threat to democratic societies. To what extent do you agree?
الاستقطاب السياسي المتصاعد تهديد للمجتمعات الديمقراطية. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
Political polarisation — a phenomenon in which citizens increasingly sort into hostile camps whose members share neither facts nor values — does represent a genuine threat to democratic function. Democracies in which compromise is impossible become ungovernable; legislatures whose members see opponents as enemies rather than rivals can pass no meaningful legislation. That said, I would argue that the greater threat lies not in disagreement itself but in the decay of shared institutions through which disagreement was once managed. It is not difference of opinion that damages democracy — disagreement, after all, is democracy's raw material. It is the collapse of common ground in which that disagreement can be conducted honestly that poses the real danger.
