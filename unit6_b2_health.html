الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات أكاديمية عن الصحة والطب
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Discourse Markers في IELTS Writing

  • افتح الفقرة الثانية بـ: "That said, the evidence also shows…" أو "Nevertheless, critics argue…"
  • للنتيجة المنطقية: "Consequently / Hence / As a result, access to treatment has improved."
  • للتوضيح: "In other words / To put it another way, lifestyle IS medicine."
  • للتأكيد: "Above all / Crucially / Indeed, prevention is cheaper than cure."
Governments should invest more in preventive medicine rather than treatment. To what extent do you agree?
يجب أن تستثمر الحكومات في الطب الوقائي أكثر من العلاج. إلى أي مدى توافق؟ (Opinion)
The case for preventive medicine is compelling, and I largely agree that it deserves greater investment. Chronic illness now accounts for the majority of deaths and healthcare costs worldwide; consequently, treating conditions after they develop is both expensive and, in many cases, avoidable. Preventive interventions — vaccination, lifestyle education, early screening — are demonstrably cost-effective. That said, prevention is not a complete answer. Nevertheless, it addresses the causes rather than the symptoms of ill health. Furthermore, the social determinants of health — poverty, housing, education — lie beyond the reach of medicine alone. Above all, what a shift toward prevention requires is not merely funding but a political culture willing to invest in outcomes that appear decades later. I agree, therefore, that prevention deserves more, but not at the expense of those who are already ill.
Universal healthcare is a right, not a privilege. Do you agree or disagree?
الرعاية الصحية الشاملة حق لا امتياز. هل توافق؟ (Opinion)
I strongly agree that healthcare should be a right. Admittedly, the costs of universal systems are real and substantial; nevertheless, countries that have adopted them consistently show better population health outcomes at lower per-capita cost than those relying on private insurance. In other words, universal healthcare is not only fairer — it is more efficient. The argument that individuals should bear their own healthcare costs ignores a fundamental reality: illness is rarely chosen. Consequently, denying treatment based on ability to pay amounts to a punishment for misfortune. That being said, no system is perfect; even universal models face challenges of underfunding and inequality in access. Above all, the goal should be a system in which where you are born does not determine how long you live or how much you suffer.
The rise of antibiotic resistance is one of the greatest threats to global health. What are its causes and what can be done?
ما أسباب مقاومة المضادات الحيوية وما الحلول؟ (Problem/Solution)
Antibiotic resistance — the process by which bacteria evolve to survive drugs designed to kill them — has been building for decades. The primary cause is overuse: antibiotics are routinely prescribed for viral infections they cannot treat and are extensively used in industrial agriculture. Consequently, the bacteria most exposed to them have evolved resistance. Furthermore, pharmaceutical companies have largely abandoned the development of new antibiotics, as the financial returns do not justify the investment. To put it another way, the market has failed to solve a public health crisis it helped to create. Solutions require international coordination: restricting agricultural antibiotic use, incentivising new drug development, and educating both patients and prescribers. Nevertheless, the window for action is narrowing. Above all, what is required is political will at a global level — a resource that, admittedly, remains in short supply.
Advances in technology are transforming healthcare. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages.
ما مزايا وعيوب التقنية في الرعاية الصحية؟ (Advantages/Disadvantages)
Technology is reshaping medicine at a pace that is both exciting and unsettling. Telemedicine, for instance, has extended specialist access to remote communities; consequently, patients who previously waited months for a consultation can be seen within hours. Furthermore, genomic medicine is enabling diagnoses that would have been impossible a generation ago. That said, the disadvantages are significant. Not all patients can navigate digital platforms; hence, technological healthcare risks widening the health inequality it might otherwise reduce. Moreover, the collection of sensitive health data creates new privacy vulnerabilities. Admittedly, no previous medical advance has been without risk. Nevertheless, the solution is not to slow progress but to govern it carefully — ensuring that the benefits reach those who need them most, and that the dangers are anticipated rather than discovered after harm has occurred.
Mental health is as important as physical health, but receives far less attention and funding. To what extent do you agree?
الصحة النفسية بأهمية الجسدية لكن تحصل على اهتمام أقل. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
I strongly agree with both claims. Mental health conditions are among the leading causes of disability worldwide; nevertheless, mental health services receive a fraction of the funding directed at physical illness. This disparity reflects a deeper cultural problem: the stigma surrounding mental illness persists even among those who intellectually reject it. Consequently, people with conditions such as depression or anxiety delay seeking help, often until the condition has become severe. That said, there are signs of change. Furthermore, the pandemic brought mental health into mainstream conversation in ways that may prove lasting. In other words, the crisis had a silver lining. Above all, what is needed is not simply more funding — though that is urgently required — but a genuine cultural shift in which seeking help for mental illness is treated with the same matter-of-fact seriousness as seeking help for a broken bone.
