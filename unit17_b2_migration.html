الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات عن الهجرة والانتماء
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Wish Structures في IELTS Writing

  • للأسف على الحاضر: "Many migrants wish their qualifications were recognised in the host country."
  • للأسف على الماضي: "Refugees often wish they had had more time to prepare before leaving."
  • للانتقاد / الرغبة في التغيير: "Migrants wish governments would create faster integration pathways."
  • If only (أقوى): "If only host societies would see migration as an asset rather than a burden."
  • It's high time: "It's high time that migration policy reflected evidence rather than political fear."
Immigration is beneficial to the economy of the receiving country. To what extent do you agree?
الهجرة مفيدة لاقتصاد البلد المستقبِل. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The economic evidence in favour of immigration is considerably stronger than public debate often suggests. Migrants fill labour market gaps that domestic workforces cannot — in both high-skilled sectors, such as medicine and technology, and lower-skilled ones, such as agriculture and social care. Fiscal analyses in most receiving countries consistently show that migrants, particularly working-age ones, contribute more in taxes than they consume in public services. If only policymakers would communicate this evidence more effectively, anti-immigration sentiment might be better tempered by fact. That said, the benefits are not automatically shared equally: if they were, communities experiencing rapid demographic change might wish their concerns had been taken more seriously earlier. It is high time that migration policy was designed to ensure host communities benefit alongside new arrivals, rather than assuming that aggregate economic gains will distribute themselves equitably without deliberate intervention. I largely agree that immigration is economically beneficial — but only when managed with adequate investment in integration and public services.
Governments should do more to help refugees integrate into society. To what extent do you agree?
يجب على الحكومات فعل المزيد لمساعدة اللاجئين على الاندماج. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
I strongly agree. Refugees — by definition, people who have fled persecution, conflict, or disaster — arrive in receiving countries with needs that go beyond the provision of safety. If integration is to succeed, language acquisition, credential recognition, employment support, and mental health services are all required. Many refugees wish they had been able to access these services earlier in the settlement process; the evidence suggests that early investment in integration produces far greater long-term returns — economic and social — than delayed or minimal support. If only host governments would treat integration as an investment rather than a cost, the outcomes for both refugees and receiving societies would improve substantially. It is high time that the international community moved beyond treating refugee support as a humanitarian emergency and began treating it as a long-term development challenge requiring sustained, evidence-based commitment.
People who migrate to a new country should fully adopt its culture. To what extent do you agree?
من يهاجر إلى بلد جديد يجب أن يتبنّى ثقافته بالكامل. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The claim that migrants should fully adopt the culture of their new country rests on a misunderstanding of how cultural identity works. I wish proponents of full assimilation would acknowledge that cultural identity is not a garment to be removed and replaced — it is deeply embedded in language, memory, family, and selfhood. What integration realistically and legitimately requires is not cultural replacement but civic participation: respecting the laws and institutions of the host society, engaging with its public life, and developing sufficient linguistic competence to do so. If only integration policies focused on these achievable civic goals rather than cultural conformity, they would be both more effective and more respectful. I largely disagree with the premise: multicultural societies that allow migrants to maintain their heritage while participating fully in civic life tend to demonstrate greater social cohesion, not less.
Brain drain is one of the most serious problems caused by migration. To what extent do you agree?
هجرة الأدمغة من أشد مشاكل الهجرة خطورةً. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
Brain drain — the emigration of highly educated and skilled workers from lower-income to higher-income countries — represents a genuine and serious challenge. Countries that invest in the education of doctors, engineers, and teachers only to see them leave for better salaries elsewhere pay a real and measurable cost. I wish origin countries could offer conditions that would make emigration less attractive to their most qualified citizens: competitive salaries, functioning institutions, and the rule of law are what skilled professionals most commonly say they wish they had had at home. If only the international community took brain drain as seriously as it takes trade imbalances, compensatory frameworks might be established. However, the picture is more complex than the drain metaphor suggests: diaspora communities often send remittances, invest in home economies, and return with international experience. It is high time that 'brain drain' was reframed as 'brain circulation' — provided that the conditions for genuine return are created.
Multiculturalism has failed in many Western countries. To what extent do you agree?
التعددية الثقافية فشلت في كثير من الدول الغربية. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The claim that multiculturalism has 'failed' depends entirely on what multiculturalism was supposed to achieve and how success is measured. If success means the elimination of inter-community tension and full social harmony, then it has not succeeded — but neither has any other social policy. If it means the peaceful coexistence of multiple cultural communities within a shared civic framework, the evidence is considerably more positive than the rhetoric of failure suggests. I wish politicians who declare the death of multiculturalism would acknowledge the cities — London, Toronto, Amsterdam, Melbourne — that function as highly diverse, broadly cohesive societies. If only the debate were conducted with this complexity rather than treating all social tension as evidence of multicultural failure. That said, it is high time that multiculturalism as a policy acknowledged its own shortcomings: communities can be multicultural and still contain deep inequalities, persistent discrimination, and insufficient investment in shared civic life. The problem is not diversity — it is the failure to build the institutions that make diversity work.
