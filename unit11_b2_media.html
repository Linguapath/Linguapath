الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات أكاديمية عن الإعلام والتأثير
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Reduced Relative Clauses في IELTS Writing

  • بدل "stories which are told by powerful groups shape reality" → "stories told by powerful groups shape reality"
  • بدل "content that is shared on social media reaches millions" → "content shared on social media reaches millions"
  • بدل "journalists who are working in conflict zones face unique risks" → "journalists working in conflict zones face unique risks"
  • تجعل الجمل أكثر إيجازاً وأكاديمية في IELTS Task 2.
The media has too much power in shaping public opinion. To what extent do you agree?
الإعلام يمتلك قوة مفرطة في تشكيل الرأي العام. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The media does exercise enormous influence, and I broadly agree that this influence is insufficiently scrutinised. Stories told from a single perspective become the lens through which audiences understand entire populations. Narratives constructed by a handful of dominant outlets shape what citizens consider important, urgent, or threatening. That said, it is possible to overstate the case. Audiences receiving information today have access to a greater variety of sources than at any previous point in history. Content shared online can bypass traditional gatekeepers, and citizens empowered by digital tools can challenge accounts published by established media. Nevertheless, the concern raised by critics of media power is legitimate: when a small number of companies control the platforms used by billions, the potential for influence — whether exercised deliberately or through structural bias — is profound.
Social media does more harm than good. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
وسائل التواصل الاجتماعي تضرّ أكثر مما تنفع. ناقش كلا الرأيَين.
Social media has delivered genuine benefits: voices previously excluded from public discourse now reach global audiences; information shared in real time has coordinated humanitarian responses; and communities built around shared interests provide connection and support to isolated individuals. That said, the harms documented by researchers studying social media's effects are substantial. Filter bubbles created by algorithmic curation mean that users see content reinforcing existing beliefs rather than challenging them. Misinformation spreading faster than correction reshapes public understanding of events. Moreover, the business model underlying most platforms profits from outrage — content designed to provoke strong emotion, however divisive, generates more engagement. On balance, I believe social media's potential is real but its current design is harmful. The problem is not the technology but the incentives structuring how it operates.
Freedom of the press is essential for democracy. To what extent do you agree?
حرية الصحافة ضرورية للديمقراطية. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
A free press is not merely one feature of democracy among many — it is among its essential preconditions. Governments operating without scrutiny applied by independent journalists invariably abuse the power entrusted to them. Stories published at personal risk by investigative reporters have repeatedly exposed corruption, illegality, and abuse that official channels were designed to conceal. That said, freedom of the press is not an uncomplicated good. Media organisations owned by political or commercial interests may exercise their freedom selectively, suppressing inconvenient truths while amplifying convenient ones. A press free from government interference but captured by wealthy owners may serve those owners rather than the public. The solution is not less press freedom but more diverse ownership and stronger protections for journalists working in the public interest.
People should be responsible for checking whether information is true before sharing it. To what extent do you agree?
يجب أن يتحقق الناس من صحة المعلومات قبل مشاركتها. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
I agree that individuals bear responsibility for the information they spread — sharing content without verification contributes to the pollution of the information environment used by everyone. That said, placing the burden entirely on individuals is both unrealistic and unjust. Misinformation crafted to appear credible can deceive even careful readers. The volume of content consumed daily makes thorough fact-checking impossible for most people. Furthermore, the platforms designed to maximise sharing create structural incentives against pausing to verify. Content shared emotionally and instantly generates the engagement that platforms reward; content checked slowly and shared cautiously does not. In my view, individual responsibility matters, but platform design, algorithmic accountability, and media literacy education bear equal if not greater responsibility for the quality of information circulating in public life.
Advertising manipulates rather than informs consumers. To what extent do you agree?
الإعلانات تُلاعب المستهلكين بدلاً من إعلامهم. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The honest answer is that advertising does both — and the balance between information and manipulation has shifted dramatically toward the latter in the digital age. In principle, advertising informs consumers about products available to them, enabling more efficient choices. In practice, techniques refined over decades exploit cognitive biases documented by psychologists: scarcity signals triggering anxiety, social proof manufactured to suggest consensus, and aspirational imagery designed to create dissatisfaction with the present. Advertising targeted using personal data harvested without full consent operates at a granularity of manipulation that traditional advertising never achieved. That said, calling all advertising manipulative overstates the case. Informative advertising — announcing a new product's features, comparing prices, communicating a service's availability — serves consumers genuinely. The problem lies in the portion of advertising that persuades through emotion, distortion, or exploitation of psychological vulnerability rather than through honest information.
