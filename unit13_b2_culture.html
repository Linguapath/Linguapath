الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات عن الثقافة والهوية
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Hedging Advanced في IELTS Writing

  • للحجج المحتملة: "It could be argued that cultural identity is increasingly fluid."
  • للادعاءات المُقيَّدة: "Cultural values tend to persist across generations, though not unchanged."
  • للتأهيل: "To some extent, globalisation homogenises culture — but not entirely."
  • للشك المُسنَد: "There is reason to believe that language shapes thought rather than merely expressing it."
Cultural identity is becoming less important in a globalised world. To what extent do you agree?
الهوية الثقافية تفقد أهميتها في عالم مُعوْلَم. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
It could be argued that globalisation has weakened the distinctiveness of local cultures, as the same brands, media, and fashions spread across societies once separated by geography and tradition. There is reason to believe that younger generations, in particular, tend to construct identities that draw on multiple cultural sources simultaneously. To some extent, this represents a genuine erosion of local distinctiveness. However, I would largely disagree that cultural identity is becoming less important. The evidence suggests that globalisation has, in many cases, provoked a renewed assertion of cultural identity rather than its dissolution. People appear to value their distinctive cultural heritage more intensely when it feels threatened. It would seem, therefore, that the relationship between globalisation and cultural identity is more paradoxical than straightforward: the former tends to intensify rather than extinguish the latter.
Children who grow up speaking more than one language have significant advantages. To what extent do you agree?
الأطفال الذين يكبرون وهم يتحدثون أكثر من لغة يمتلكون مزايا كبيرة. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The research evidence tends to support the view that bilingualism confers genuine cognitive advantages: bilingual children appear to develop stronger executive function, including better attention control and the ability to switch between tasks. There is reason to believe that managing two linguistic systems simultaneously exercises cognitive capacities that transfer to non-linguistic domains. Furthermore, the cultural advantages could be argued to be equally significant: access to multiple cultural worlds through their original languages gives children perspectives that monolingual peers typically lack. To some extent, the advantages are conditional — on maintaining both languages actively rather than allowing one to atrophy. The picture is, however, largely positive. It would seem that the question is not whether bilingualism helps, but how societies can better support it, particularly for children of migrant families who may risk losing heritage languages under social pressure to assimilate.
Governments should do more to protect endangered languages. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
يجب على الحكومات فعل المزيد لحماية اللغات المُهدَّدة بالانقراض. ناقش.
There is reason to believe that every language that disappears takes with it an irreplaceable way of understanding the world. Languages tend to encode concepts, relationships, and knowledge that have no equivalent in dominant tongues; linguists could be argued to be right in comparing language loss to the extinction of a species. From this perspective, the case for government intervention seems compelling. Those who oppose such intervention typically argue that languages die because their speakers choose not to use them — and that this represents a legitimate exercise of individual and community agency. To some extent, this objection has merit. It would seem, however, that such choices are rarely made freely when the alternative is social marginalisation. I believe that support for endangered languages — through education, broadcasting, and community funding — is justified, provided it is pursued in dialogue with the communities concerned rather than imposed over their heads.
Immigration enriches the culture of the receiving country. To what extent do you agree?
الهجرة تُثري ثقافة البلد المُستقبِل. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
It could be argued that every society currently celebrated for cultural richness — the United States, the United Kingdom, France — is the product of successive waves of migration. There is reason to believe that exposure to different cultural practices, cuisines, artistic traditions, and ways of thinking tends to produce more creative and adaptive societies. The evidence for cultural enrichment through immigration is, in many respects, compelling. To some extent, however, the picture is more complex. Cultural exchange appears to work best when it occurs on terms of genuine equality, rather than when migrants are expected to assimilate entirely while receiving limited recognition of what they bring. It would seem that the question is not whether immigration enriches culture — it demonstrably does — but whether societies are structured to recognise and benefit from that enrichment equitably.
Traditional values and modern lifestyles are fundamentally incompatible. To what extent do you agree?
القيم التقليدية وأنماط الحياة الحديثة متعارضة أساساً. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
It could be argued that the claim of fundamental incompatibility rests on a misunderstanding of how cultures actually work. Traditions tend to evolve continuously rather than persisting unchanged; what appears traditional today was, in many cases, itself an innovation in a previous era. There is reason to believe that the most resilient cultural communities are those that adapt selectively — maintaining values that remain meaningful while releasing practices that have become burdensome. To some extent, tensions between traditional expectations and modern possibilities are real: particularly around gender roles, family structure, and religious observance. However, these tensions appear to be navigated successfully by millions of people who would not describe themselves as having abandoned tradition. It would seem, therefore, that incompatibility is not inherent but situational — a product of specific choices about which values to hold onto and which to revise.
