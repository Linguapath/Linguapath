الكتابة

اكتب عن الحياة الحضرية وتخطيط المدن
Describe the city you live in or grew up in. Use articles correctly.
صف المدينة التي تعيش فيها أو نشأت فيها. استخدم المقالات بشكل صحيح.
The city I grew up in was built primarily for cars rather than people. A typical day required driving everywhere — the market, the school, even the nearest park. Public transport barely existed. Walking felt dangerous rather than pleasurable. Cities like this are common, built in an era when the car seemed like progress.
What does your ideal neighbourhood look like?
كيف يبدو حيّك المثالي؟
An ideal neighbourhood combines housing with the shops, cafes, and public spaces that make spontaneous community possible. The streets should be designed for people walking, not cars speeding. A park within five minutes' walk is essential. The neighbourhood should have a mix of generations and backgrounds — homogeneity tends to produce the social isolation that modern cities increasingly suffer from.
Is rapid urban development good or bad for a city's character?
هل التطوير الحضري السريع جيد أم سيء لطابع المدينة؟
The rapid development of a city often destroys the very character that made it attractive. A neighbourhood that takes a century to develop cannot be replicated in a decade. The heritage of a place — its layered history, its diverse communities — takes time to accumulate. Development that ignores this tends to produce cities that look modern but feel empty.
How could your city be improved in the next ten years?
كيف يمكن تحسين مدينتك في السنوات العشر القادمة؟
The most important investment the city could make is in public transport. A reliable metro system connecting the main residential areas to the centre would reduce car dependency and improve air quality. The green spaces that exist should be expanded and connected. A network of tree-lined streets linking parks would transform the walkability of the whole city at relatively low cost.
Do you prefer city life or rural life? Why?
هل تُفضّل الحياة الحضرية أم الريفية؟ لماذا؟
The city offers a density of experience that the countryside cannot match — the variety of food, culture, ideas, and people within walking distance is genuinely extraordinary. However, the countryside provides something cities rarely offer: a relationship with the natural world that restores something essential. The ideal would be a small city dense enough to be walkable but surrounded by accessible nature.
