الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات أكاديمية عن إصلاح التعليم
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Stance Adverbials في IELTS Writing

  • للرأي المحتمل: "Arguably, standardised testing does more harm than good."
  • للتأكيد: "Crucially, teacher quality matters more than class size."
  • للمفاجأة: "Surprisingly, some of the best-educated nations spend less per pupil."
  • للتعميم: "Broadly speaking, rote learning fails to develop critical thinking."
  • للتصريح: "Frankly, the curriculum has not changed significantly in a century."
Standardised testing is the best way to measure students' ability. To what extent do you agree?
الاختبارات الموحّدة هي أفضل طريقة لقياس قدرات الطلاب. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
Standardised testing has, admittedly, genuine merits: it provides comparable data across schools and systems, and it ensures that assessment is not left entirely to the discretion of individual teachers. That said, I largely disagree that it constitutes the best measure of ability. Crucially, what standardised tests measure well is a narrow range of skills — recall, speed, and the ability to perform under pressure. Arguably, they systematically disadvantage students whose strengths lie in creativity, collaboration, or practical intelligence. Surprisingly, some of the world's highest-performing education systems — Finland being the most cited example — rely minimally on standardised testing. Broadly speaking, the most important question is not how to measure students more efficiently, but what we want education to achieve. Frankly, if the goal is merely to produce good test scores, we have already failed.
Should education be free at all levels? Discuss both views and give your opinion.
هل يجب أن يكون التعليم مجانياً في جميع المراحل؟ ناقش كلا الرأيَين.
Undoubtedly, free education at primary and secondary level is a cornerstone of social mobility and democratic participation. The case for free university education is more complex. Arguably, the public benefits of a highly educated workforce justify the public cost. Crucially, when university education is accessible only to those who can afford it, talent is wasted on a massive scale — an outcome that is economically as well as morally damaging. Those who oppose universal free higher education rightly note that graduates typically earn significantly more over their lifetimes; consequently, some contribution seems fair. That said, debt-based funding systems demonstrably deter students from disadvantaged backgrounds, often those with the most to gain. Frankly, a society that allows wealth to determine who reaches the highest levels of education is one that has surrendered the idea of meritocracy. In my view, free education at all levels is not a luxury but a long-term economic investment.
Technology is transforming education. What are the advantages and disadvantages?
التقنية تُحوّل التعليم. ما مزاياه وعيوبه؟
Technology has, arguably, done more to democratise access to knowledge than any previous innovation. A student in a remote village with an internet connection can, remarkably, now access lectures from the world's finest universities at no cost. Crucially, adaptive learning platforms can personalise instruction in ways no single teacher could manage for thirty students simultaneously. The disadvantages, however, are equally real. Surprisingly, research on screen-based learning suggests that comprehension and retention are often lower than with print. Furthermore, technology-dependent education risks widening the digital divide, disadvantaging those without reliable internet access. Broadly speaking, the most effective educational technology is not that which replaces teachers but that which gives skilled teachers better tools. Frankly, the evidence suggests that a good teacher with limited technology will outperform a poor one with every gadget available.
Teachers are the most important factor in a child's education. Do you agree?
المعلمون هم أهم عامل في تعليم الطفل. هل توافق؟
The evidence strongly suggests that teacher quality is among the most powerful determinants of educational outcomes. Crucially, research consistently shows that the difference between an excellent teacher and a poor one translates into years of additional learning progress for students. Undoubtedly, a child whose formative years are spent with engaged, skilled teachers gains advantages that persist throughout their life. That said, it would be overstating the case to claim teachers are the sole important factor. Arguably, socioeconomic background, parental involvement, and peer culture exercise comparable influence — particularly outside the classroom. Surprisingly, school funding, while politically significant, shows a weaker correlation with outcomes than teacher quality once basic resources are available. In my view, the single most impactful educational reform any government could make is to raise the status, training, and pay of teachers — making the profession genuinely competitive with law, medicine, and finance.
Schools should teach practical life skills as well as academic subjects. To what extent do you agree?
يجب أن تُدرّس المدارس مهارات الحياة العملية إلى جانب المواد الأكاديمية. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
I strongly agree that schools should expand their curriculum to include practical life skills. Frankly, a student who can solve a quadratic equation but cannot manage a budget, cook a nutritious meal, or identify the symptoms of poor mental health has been, arguably, only partially educated. Crucially, these are not soft extras to be squeezed in after the real curriculum — they are foundational to adult functioning. The objection that schools already have too little time for academic content is understandable. However, broadly speaking, the countries with the most effective education systems have found ways to integrate practical skills without sacrificing academic rigour. Remarkably, evidence from Finland and Singapore — consistently among the world's top performers — suggests that a broader curriculum and more student autonomy can actually improve, not diminish, academic results. Undoubtedly, the ultimate goal of education is to produce capable adults, not examination-ready adolescents.
