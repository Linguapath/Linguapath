الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات أكاديمية عن العلم والتقنية
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 كيف تدرّب نفسك للكتابة

  • اكتب ٢٥٠+ كلمة خلال ٤٠ دقيقة بالضبط — وقّت نفسك دائماً
  • البنية: مقدمة (أعد صياغة السؤال + موقفك) ← فقرتا جسم (فكرة + شرح + مثال) ← خاتمة
  • استخدم الـ Impersonal Structures — ترفع درجة Grammatical Range وتعطي نبرة أكاديمية محايدة
  • استخدم collocations الوحدة (scientific evidence, peer review…) لرفع Lexical Resource
Some people believe scientific research should be funded by governments rather than private companies. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
يرى البعض أن البحث العلمي ينبغي أن تموّله الحكومات لا الشركات الخاصة. إلى أي مدى توافق؟ (Opinion essay)
It is often argued that government funding keeps science independent, and to a large extent I agree. When research is financed privately, it is feared that profit, not truth, sets the agenda. It is widely accepted that breakthroughs in basic science — the kind with no immediate commercial value — are precisely those the market will not support. That said, it cannot be denied that private funding drives rapid innovation. It would seem, therefore, that the wisest system combines public funding for fundamental research with private investment for its application.
Some argue the benefits of artificial intelligence outweigh its risks, while others disagree. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
ناقش الرأيين حول فوائد ومخاطر الذكاء الاصطناعي وأعطِ رأيك. (Discuss both views)
It is predicted that AI will transform medicine, transport, and education, and supporters point to these gains as decisive. It is also claimed that automation will free people from dangerous and repetitive work. Critics, however, warn of unintended consequences: it is feared that millions of jobs will disappear before new ones emerge. In my view, the technology is neither good nor bad in itself. It is increasingly recognised that the real question is one of regulation and accountability — and it would seem that we are answering it too slowly.
What are the advantages and disadvantages of rapid technological progress?
ما مزايا وعيوب التقدّم التقني السريع؟ (Advantages / disadvantages)
The advantages of rapid progress are obvious. It is widely accepted that new technologies raise living standards, cure disease, and connect the world. Yet the disadvantages are equally real. It is frequently observed that progress brings unintended consequences, from pollution to misinformation, and it has been suggested that change now outpaces our ability to regulate it. It is worth noting that what is cutting-edge today is obsolete tomorrow. On balance, progress is welcome — provided that ethics and oversight are not left behind.
Misinformation about science is spreading rapidly. What are the causes, and what can be done?
المعلومات المضللة حول العلم تنتشر بسرعة. ما الأسباب وما الحلول؟ (Problem / solution)
It is feared that misinformation now spreads faster than any correction. Several causes can be identified: social media rewards drama over accuracy, and it is often forgotten that scientific consensus rests on evidence, not on confident assertion. It has also been suggested that low scientific literacy leaves the public vulnerable. As for solutions, it is widely agreed that education is the foundation. It is argued, further, that transparency from scientists and accountability from platforms are both essential. Only when good information is easier to find than bad will the problem ease.
Some say traditional skills are becoming obsolete because of technology. Is this a positive or negative development?
يقول البعض إن المهارات التقليدية تندثر بسبب التقنية. هل هذا تطور إيجابي أم سلبي؟ (Direct question)
It is undeniable that technology is making some skills obsolete; few would now learn to navigate by the stars. On balance, I regard this as positive, though not without cost. It is generally accepted that automation frees us to focus on higher tasks, and it is argued that every generation has feared the loss of skills it later did not miss. Yet it is worth noting that something is lost when a craft disappears entirely. It would seem, then, that the goal should be to preserve what is valuable while embracing what is genuinely better.
