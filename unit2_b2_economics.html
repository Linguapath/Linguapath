الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات أكاديمية عن الاقتصاد والأسواق
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 كيف تدرّب نفسك — الاقتصاد في IELTS

  • ابدأ مقدمتك بـ Inversion: "Never has the gap between rich and poor been wider…"
  • استخدم inversions في الجسم لإضافة التأثير: "Not only does inequality harm growth, but it also undermines trust."
  • الـ Inversion يرفع درجة Grammatical Range مباشرة — الممتحن يلاحظه
  • وقّت نفسك: ٤٠ دقيقة بالضبط، ٢٥٠+ كلمة
The gap between rich and poor is growing in many countries. What are the causes of this, and what can governments do to address it?
تتّسع الفجوة بين الأغنياء والفقراء في دول كثيرة. ما أسباب ذلك وما الذي تستطيع الحكومات فعله؟ (Problem/Solution)
Never has the wealth gap been wider than it is today, and several forces are driving it. Not only has automation displaced low-skilled workers, but it has also concentrated profits among those who own the technology. Hardly had globalisation raised living standards for some before it began hollowing out middle-income employment in others. As for solutions, governments can raise minimum wages, invest in education, and reform tax systems so that capital is taxed as heavily as labour. Under no circumstances should the state abandon those left behind by economic change. Only through a combination of redistribution and investment will the gap begin to narrow.
Some economists argue that free markets are the best way to organise an economy. Others believe governments must intervene. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
ناقش رأيَي السوق الحر مقابل تدخّل الدولة وأعطِ رأيك. (Discuss both views)
Rarely do economists agree on much, but the debate between free markets and state intervention has generated more heat than most. Proponents of free markets argue that only through competition can productivity rise and prices fall. Not only does intervention distort incentives, they say, but it also creates inefficiency. Yet the opposing view is equally compelling. Hardly had the financial crisis of 2008 begun before even committed free-marketeers accepted the need for government bailouts. In my view, neither extreme is wise. Only a market tempered by sensible regulation and a safety net for the vulnerable can produce both growth and fairness.
What are the advantages and disadvantages of economic globalisation?
ما مزايا وعيوب العولمة الاقتصادية؟ (Advantages/Disadvantages)
Not only has globalisation lifted millions out of poverty, but it has also given consumers access to cheaper goods than ever before. Under no circumstances should these gains be dismissed. Yet the disadvantages are real. Seldom have the benefits of trade been distributed evenly; not only do some regions prosper, but others suffer prolonged unemployment as industries migrate. Little do critics of globalisation realise, however, that closing borders rarely brings back the jobs that left. On balance, globalisation is a force for good — but only if its gains are redistributed through strong social policies and investment in workers displaced by change.
High inflation causes serious problems for society. To what extent do you agree?
التضخم المرتفع يسبب مشاكل خطيرة للمجتمع. إلى أي مدى توافق؟ (Opinion)
Rarely does an economic problem affect every citizen as immediately as inflation, and to a large extent I agree that its consequences are serious. Not only does rising inflation erode purchasing power, but it also punishes those on fixed incomes most cruelly. Hardly had inflation peaked in 2022 before millions found they could no longer afford basic goods. That said, a degree of nuance is required: not only is low, stable inflation considered healthy by most economists, but deflation — its opposite — carries its own dangers. Only when inflation becomes persistent and unpredictable does it truly destabilise an economy and erode public confidence.
Some say that economic growth should be the main priority of governments. Others argue that protecting the environment is more important. Discuss.
هل الأولوية للنمو الاقتصادي أم حماية البيئة؟ (Discuss both views)
Never has the tension between growth and environmental protection been more acute. Advocates of growth argue that only through a strong economy can a society afford clean energy and environmental protection. Not only does poverty harm people directly, they say, but it also prevents the investment in green technology that sustainability requires. The opposing view holds that under no circumstances should short-term GDP growth be prioritised over the long-term survival of the planet. Hardly had scientists issued their most urgent climate warnings before governments continued approving new fossil fuel projects. In my view, only a model of sustainable development — which treats ecological limits as non-negotiable — can resolve this conflict.
