الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات أكاديمية عن البيئة والمناخ
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Fronting في IELTS Writing

  • افتح المقدمة بـ cleft: "It is not technology that is lacking — it is political will."
  • استخدم What-cleft في الجسم: "What the data shows is an undeniable trend."
  • افتح الفقرة بـ fronted adverbial: "Threatened by rising seas, millions face displacement."
  • استخدم emphatic do لتأكيد الحقائق: "Governments do have the tools — what they lack is commitment."
Some people believe that individuals can do little to protect the environment. Others argue that individual action is essential. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
هل يستطيع الأفراد فعل الكثير لحماية البيئة؟ (Discuss both views)
What the sceptics argue is that individual choices — recycling, reducing meat, choosing public transport — are structurally insignificant compared to industrial emissions. It is corporations and governments, they insist, that bear the real responsibility. This view has merit. With fossil fuel companies accounting for the majority of global emissions, individual action does seem limited in scale. That said, what individual choices do is shift culture and signal demand. It is not the action itself that matters most, but the political pressure it generates. Threatened by voter apathy, governments will not act; galvanised by public will, they can and do. What we need is both structural change and individual engagement — not one at the expense of the other.
Economic development and environmental protection are often seen as conflicting goals. To what extent do you agree?
هل النمو الاقتصادي وحماية البيئة أهداف متعارضة؟ (Opinion)
What history suggests is that growth and environmental protection have often conflicted — but what the twenty-first century demands is that we reject this framing. It is the old model of growth that is incompatible with a stable climate, not growth itself. With renewable technology now cost-competitive with fossil fuels, the economic case for clean development is stronger than ever. Threatened by ecological collapse, economies face far greater long-term costs than those of transition today. What the circular economy offers is precisely a model in which prosperity and sustainability are not in tension. I largely disagree, therefore, that these goals are inherently conflicting — they are only so if we refuse to innovate.
What are the causes of climate change, and who is responsible for addressing it?
ما أسباب تغيّر المناخ ومن المسؤول عن معالجته؟ (Problem/Solution)
What drives climate change is the burning of fossil fuels, which releases greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. Deforestation compounds the problem, as does industrial agriculture. With emissions having risen every decade since industrialisation, the cause is not mysterious — it is the global economy's dependence on carbon. As for responsibility, it is the world's largest economies that must lead — historically, it is they who produced the majority of cumulative emissions. That said, what developing nations demand is fair access to the clean technology needed to grow without repeating historical mistakes. What the Paris Agreement attempted was precisely this global bargain. What remains missing is the finance to make it real.
Some argue that nuclear energy is necessary to address climate change. Do you agree or disagree?
هل الطاقة النووية ضرورية لمعالجة تغيّر المناخ؟ (Opinion)
What the climate crisis demands is a rapid reduction in carbon emissions — and it is this requirement that makes nuclear energy worth reconsidering. With solar and wind alone unable to guarantee base-load power, the case for nuclear is stronger than its critics allow. It is not the technology that is most dangerous — it is the ideology that rules it out before examining the evidence. That said, what concerns many is the unresolved problem of nuclear waste and the risk of catastrophic failure. What I would argue is not that nuclear is ideal, but that no energy source is — and that, threatened by climate collapse, we cannot afford to exclude any low-carbon option on principle.
Deforestation is one of the greatest threats to the environment. What are its causes and effects, and what should be done?
إزالة الغابات من أكبر التهديدات البيئية. ما أسبابها وآثارها والحلول؟ (Causes/Effects/Solutions)
What drives deforestation is primarily agricultural demand — it is the global appetite for beef and soy that clears the greatest area of forest each year. With forests absorbing approximately thirty percent of human carbon emissions, their destruction accelerates the very crisis they could help to slow. Threatened by habitat loss, thousands of species face extinction, and indigenous communities lose the lands on which they depend. What must change is not just policy but economics: it is currently more profitable to clear forest than to protect it. What governments can do is make conservation financially competitive through carbon credits and international payments. It is the political will to enforce such systems, not the systems themselves, that is most urgently needed.
