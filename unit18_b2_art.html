الكتابة — IELTS Writing Task 2

مقالات عن الفن والأدب والإبداع
IELTS · TASK 2

📋 Ellipsis في IELTS Writing

  • "Art communicates what language cannot, and music does so even more directly."
  • "Some argue that art should be accessible to all; others, that quality requires exclusivity."
  • "She painted in oils and he in watercolour." (gapping ellipsis)
  • "Picasso could reinvent himself — and did — multiple times in a single decade."
  • "The novel challenged assumptions that few critics had questioned before."
Art and music should be compulsory subjects in all schools. To what extent do you agree?
الفن والموسيقى يجب أن تكون مواد إلزامية في المدارس. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
I broadly agree, though with important qualifications. Art and music develop capacities that academic subjects typically do not — and cannot: the ability to tolerate ambiguity, to express what resists literal statement, and to appreciate that meaning can be made in multiple registers simultaneously. A curriculum that excludes these subjects produces graduates who are technically proficient and emotionally narrow. That said, the case for compulsion is not absolute. Art education delivered without resources, qualified teachers, or genuine commitment produces resentment rather than appreciation. Many students who might have loved drawing, painting, or playing an instrument have been deterred by poor teaching. The argument is therefore not simply for compulsory art but for art taught well — with the same seriousness of purpose and investment of resources that mathematics and science typically receive. So do I believe these subjects should be compulsory? Yes — provided the curriculum has the courage to treat them as genuinely important rather than as enrichment extras.
Governments should fund the arts even when money is scarce. To what extent do you agree?
يجب على الحكومات تمويل الفنون حتى حين تشحّ الأموال. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The arts are among the most contested objects of public spending — and rightly so, in the sense that they demand genuine justification. The case for public funding rests on several arguments, each distinct and each important. First, the arts produce public goods — cultural memory, shared identity, the civilisational record — that markets systematically underprovide. Second, art accessible only to those who can afford premium tickets is art that serves the already privileged; public subsidy ensures broader access. Third, creative industries generate economic activity and employment that depends on a cultural ecosystem sustained by public investment. Those who argue that arts spending is a luxury in hard times typically do so while defending other forms of public spending on precisely these grounds. So do I agree that governments should fund the arts even in difficult times? I do — but I would add that arts funding should be distributed equitably, not concentrated in elite institutions that primarily serve those who need public support least.
Literature is the most important of all the arts. To what extent do you agree?
الأدب أهم الفنون جميعاً. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The claim that literature is the most important of the arts is a claim that literature lovers often make — and one that music lovers, painters, and architects would each contest with equal conviction. I am sympathetic to the view that literature has a particular capacity to represent the complexity of human experience over time: a novel can inhabit multiple consciousnesses simultaneously, render the gap between what people think and what they say, and locate individual experience within historical and social forces in ways that other art forms struggle to do. So does literature have unique capacities? It does. Does that make it the most important art? That is a different question. Music communicates what language cannot; visual art presents what resists verbal description; architecture shapes the daily environment of entire populations. The comparative ranking of art forms reflects the ranker's preferences more than the arts' actual relative importance. I would rather celebrate what each does uniquely than argue for a hierarchy that serves no purpose except to diminish what it ranks below the top.
The purpose of art is to challenge society, not to entertain it. To what extent do you agree?
هدف الفن تحدّي المجتمع لا إمتاعه. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
The dichotomy between challenging and entertaining art is largely false — and the greatest works tend to do both simultaneously. Shakespeare entertained popular audiences with comedy and spectacle and, in doing so, dismantled assumptions about power, gender, and justice that his contemporaries took for granted. Mozart delighted aristocratic patrons and, in the same works, expressed a subversive humanity that transcended their social order. So does the best art entertain? It does. Does it challenge? So does it. The false dichotomy serves two equally problematic positions: those who think art should always be comfortable and those who think discomfort is evidence of artistic seriousness. I believe the purpose of art is not reducible to a single function. Art can console, disturb, commemorate, protest, celebrate, and disorient — often in the same work. What art cannot do, without ceasing to be art, is leave its audience exactly as it found them.
Digital technology has made art more accessible and therefore more valuable. To what extent do you agree?
التقنية الرقمية جعلت الفن أكثر إتاحةً وبالتالي أكثر قيمة. إلى أي مدى توافق؟
Digital technology has unquestionably democratised access to art in ways that previous generations could not have imagined. Streaming platforms give listeners access to more music than any library could hold; digital archives make literary and visual art available to anyone with an internet connection. So has technology made art more accessible? Dramatically so. Whether this has made it more valuable is a different and more contested question. Accessibility and value are not synonymous. The reproducibility of digital art has, in some respects, undermined the economic model that sustained professional artists, and the abundance of available work has not obviously deepened audience engagement — it may have done the opposite. There is a real question about whether ease of access encourages or discourages the sustained attention that art rewards. I would argue that technology has expanded the potential audience for art enormously — but that realising the value of this potential requires more than access alone. It requires the cultivation of attention, taste, and the willingness to be moved by what one did not expect.

🎙️ Ellipsis في Speaking

  • "I love painting and so does my sister." (so + auxiliary)
  • "I tried to paint but couldn't." (verb phrase ellipsis)
  • "Some people prefer classical music; others, modern jazz." (gapping)
  • "I read the book — and enjoyed it, which surprised me." (clausal ellipsis)
  • "Has she seen the exhibition? — She has." (short answer ellipsis)
